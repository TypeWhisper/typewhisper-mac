# Unreleased

- Reson8 1.1.0 (requires TypeWhisper 1.8.1): The model picker offers Rumble-2-Flash, Reson8's fast model for prerecorded audio in 45 languages, next to Resonant-1 and your custom models, which run on Resonant-1. The list comes from Reson8, so a new model appears after Refresh, and the languages and live streaming follow the selected model. Dictations with Resonant-1 now stream to Reson8 while you speak: the live preview, live text in the text field and the Live Transcript window show Reson8's transcript as it arrives, instead of a local engine's or one that uploads the last seconds again every few seconds. The final text comes from the same connection, in our tests about 0.3 seconds after a short dictation stopped; if the connection fails, TypeWhisper transcribes the whole recording as before. Recordings, file transcriptions and dictations without live preview now go to Reson8's prerecorded endpoint, which costs 1 credit per minute instead of 2 and in our tests finished a 72-second recording in 0.9 seconds instead of 2.0; Advanced settings switch them back to realtime, which shows the text while it arrives. A new Filler words setting leaves words such as "um" out of the transcript, keeps them as spoken, or leaves the choice to the model as before. With several languages selected, Reson8 detects the language of each utterance among them, and transcripts report the language it detected. Dictionary terms reach Reson8 as phrases, up to 250 per request, and Advanced settings adjust how strongly they and custom models steer recognition. With Keep dictionary in a custom model, TypeWhisper keeps the dictionary in a custom model in your Reson8 account instead and updates it when the dictionary changes, so Reson8 uses up to 50,000 terms. Pattern sets describe structured tokens such as order codes, for example AMZ[0-9]{6}; each set appears as a model, so a profile or workflow can use it where such tokens are spoken, and Check with Reson8 tests the patterns. Transcripts include word timestamps and sentence segments, which subtitles, the Recorder and speaker detection use. Speaker diarization lets Reson8 label who spoke when, up to eight speakers: with Detect Speakers on, recordings and file transcriptions use these labels instead of detecting speakers on the Mac, and dictated text stays without labels. Recordings longer than five minutes are labelled in five-minute parts, and while the option is on, transcripts arrive after the recording instead of streaming. Rumble-2-Flash does not take filler words, dictionary terms, timestamps or speaker labels, and its settings show only what it supports. Reson8's credit limit and concurrent connection limit now show Reson8's own message.
- Soniox 1.2.15: Dictation and file transcription use Soniox STT v6 (`stt-rt-v6`, `stt-async-v6`), which Soniox released on November 3, 2026; it retires v5 on December 15, 2026. A model list saved before v6 refreshes in the background when TypeWhisper starts, so **Automatic (Latest)** moves to v6 without pressing **Refresh**, and a pinned v5 model moves to **Automatic (Latest)**. In the TypeWhisper benchmark, real-time v6 makes fewer errors than v5 in English (5.6 % vs. 6.9 % WER), German (5.0 % vs. 5.5 %) and Russian (5.4 % vs. 6.3 %) and places punctuation better. v6 writes amounts with the currency as a word, for example "850 dollars" and "578.000 Euro" instead of "$850" and "578.000 €".
- ElevenLabs 1.2.0 (requires TypeWhisper 1.8.1): In Auto mode, Scribe v2 dictations now stream to ElevenLabs while you speak. The live preview, live text in the text field and the Live Transcript window show ElevenLabs' own transcript again, instead of a local engine's or none, without uploading the audio a second time. The final text comes from the same session and is ready about 0.3 seconds after you stop; in our tests a 10-second dictation took 0.3 seconds instead of 0.9, a 44-second one 0.3 seconds instead of 1.3. Dictations longer than 30 seconds stream as well instead of going to the batch API after the recording; the session commits in speech pauses about every 20 to 30 seconds. With active dictionary terms the final text still comes from the batch API, which uses the terms, because ElevenLabs' realtime API often wraps the transcript in quotation marks with them; these dictations are billed for realtime and batch when a live preview is shown. If the realtime session fails, TypeWhisper transcribes the whole recording as before. Scribe v2 Medical and REST only still transcribe after the recording, and their preview runs on a local engine.
- Watch folders: The Markdown transcript now starts with frontmatter that names the audio file, its word timestamps, the date, length, language, engine and speakers, and the text has a paragraph per speaker turn that starts with its time and speaker, for example `**[00:12:03] Speaker 1:**`. With an engine that reports word timestamps, `<file>.timestamps.json` next to the audio file holds them in the format of the HTTP API's `verbose_json` (next to the transcript when the audio file is deleted afterwards). Tools that cut audio by its transcript, such as the TypeWhisper scripts for REAPER, find a recording and the times of its words from a note in an Obsidian vault.
- Recorder: Recordings transcribed with an engine that reports word timestamps get `<recording>.timestamps.json` next to them; deleting a recording moves it to the Trash with the other files.
- Obsidian 1.3.0: Recorder notes name the recording and its word timestamps in their frontmatter (`audio:`, `timestamps:`) and show its length.
